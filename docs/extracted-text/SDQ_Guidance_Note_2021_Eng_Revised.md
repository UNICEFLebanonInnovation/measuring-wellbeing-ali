# Background

The CPiEWG needs a standard way of reporting to the Outcome 4, indicator C: % of children (boys and girls) who reported an improvement in their psychosocial wellbeing as measured through the SDQ”*.* The Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (SDQ) is a brief behavioral screening questionnaire relevant for 6-17-year-old. It exists in several versions to meet the needs of researchers, clinicians and educationalists. It includes ***25 items on psychological attributes divided into 5 scales:***

1\) emotional symptoms (5 items),  
2) conduct problems (5 items),  
3) hyperactivity/inattention (5 items),  
4) peer relationship problems (5 items),  
5) prosocial behavior (5 items).  
  
While the SDQ is often used as a diagnostic tool, for the CPiEWG purpose, it will be used to evaluate outcome. “Before” and “After” SDQs can be used to evaluate specific interventions, such as psychosocial support activities and life skills programming.

There are two validated versions of the SDQ in Arabic language:

1.  The first for age group 6-11 should be administered by facilitators[^1] with caregivers (caregivers will answer the questions for the child of age 6-11).

2.  The second version, which is for age group 12-17, can be self-administered given the child ability to read and write. It is highly recommended that even when adolescents are self-administering the questionnaire, verbal guidance should be provided by the facilitators.

**All organizations will need to use the same tool; however they can choose to use their own supplemental tools in addition to the SDQ.**

It is important to note that the SDQ (as all tools do) has limitations. Other tools should be used in order to adequately understand improvements in children’s wellbeing, including those tools that have been developed and are recommended by the PSS Committee, such as the CFS checklist and the Focus Group Discussion Guide for evaluating PSS activities with children. These tools as well as the SDQ forms and guidance note are available on the PSS Committee Dropbox and can be found here:

<https://www.dropbox.com/sh/zpfu5umh03j8m0m/AAAwt3FqOZOr7lEmI_Wk1uxEa?dl=0>

# Which activities in the 2021 LCRP require use of the SDQ

SDQ will be required for any actor conducting Focused PSS[^2] programmes for children and/or adolescents ages 6 to 17 years old which falls under the child protection LCRP Outcome 4 “Provide boys and girls at risk and survivors of violence, exploitation and abuse with access to an improved and equitable prevention and response”; Indicator 4 “*% of children (boys and girls) who reported an improvement in their psychosocial wellbeing as measured through the SDQ”*

# How to administer the SDQ

For the purposes of the CPiEWG, in order to compare results, it is important to administer the questionnaire in the same way to each age group. It is important to take consent from the caregivers and the adolescents who will participate in the questionnaire. A script is provided in the questionnaire. When you take consent from the adolescent participants for the self-administered questionnaire, ensure that you inform them that their answers are confidential, and you will not share any information with their parents/caregivers.

## Ages 6-11

The PSS Committee in Lebanon recommends that the SDQ not be administered for children under age 6, based on experience implementing the questionnaire in the Lebanese context. The preferred way to administer for age group 6-11 is to caregivers through one-on-one discussion meetings. These meetings should occur in a private space in order to preserve the confidentiality of the answers. When administering the SDQ to caregivers, it is important to “translate” the language from standard Arabic to dialect that is accessible.[^3]

| ***Tip: When caregivers register their children for the focused PSS activities, this is the ideal time to administer the pre-questionnaire**.* |
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

## Ages 12-17

For age group 12-17, the PSS Committee recommends administering the SDQ through self-administration. However, it should not be given to adolescents to fill out on their own especially if adolescents are illiterate. Instead, facilitators should read and translate the questions from Standard Arabic into dialect in order to ensure understanding of the questions. This should be done, however, in a standard way according to the terminology listed below for both age groups in a private setting.

# Who should administer the SDQ

Persons supporting the administration of the SDQ should be trained on child protection, communication with children and safe identification and referral. If organizations choose to use M&E staff to administer the SDQ, they need to be trained on the above-mentioned topics. Only one person should administer the SDQ; However, facilitator shall never be alone with the child as set per the Child Protection Minimum Standards. It is highly recommended that the same person who administered the pre-assessment should administer the post-assessment.

| If possible, it is recommended that the person who would administer the SDQ is not the same person who is running the sessions with the children to avoid biasing the answers. |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

# When to administer the SDQ

The pre-assessment should be administered with the caregivers or children/adolescents upon registration or maximum before the child/adolescent participates in the third session of the cycle. The post-assessment should be done with the caregivers or children/ adolescents after the end of the cycle, and not before, in the same manner that the pre-assessment was administered according to each organization’s system of cycles.

| **If the child doesn’t answer more than two questions, the questionnaire is considered invalid.** |
|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

# Sampling

Sampling should be done randomly, according to a sample size required which **is 200 children per organization in one specific governorate participating in Focused PSS**. The recommended sample size does not account for attrition (drop outs, not being able to find children, etc.). Therefore, the pre-assessment should be given ideally to 20% more children than the recommended sample size, meaning **240 children**. You can request as many codes as you want, and cover multiple governorates however you need to assess at least 240 children per governorate.

Codes for UNICEF partners will be allocated after discussion with UNICEF team who will ensure equal governorate representation. Therefore, and for this purpose, UNICEF will reserve the right to decide whether the additional requested data per governorate will be included in the analysis or not.

The sampling should ideally be done randomly, with a minimum of 25 sampler per location accounting for sex and age group. For example, the list of children participating in the cycle of PSS activities could first be divided by age group (6-11, 12-17) and then within the age groups by male, female. If you have 2 cycles in your calendar year from which to assess children, then you would sample 120 children per cycle. 30 males from 6-11, 30 females 6-11, 30 males 12-17, 30 females 12-17, according to your target age groups, etc.

| **If your organization cannot reach the recommended sample size, please contact the PSS Committee to discuss possible options as soon as possible.** |
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

# Coding

The system will be auto generating codes for each application that is included in the sample population.  
  
The application code is autogenerated automatically by the SDQ app, no more manual creation, each partner will have a specific set of applications per year.

## Code structure

Each Partner will have a Prefix during the addition to the system, same for the collector. Example for Partner “**Robert”** the prefix could be **ROB** and collector “**Michel”** who is a team member within Partner “Robert”, will be **MICH**. The current year of the application is “2021”

So the application auto generated code will be **Year + Partner Prefix + Collector Prefix + application ID** in this case for example It will be **2021 - ROB-MICH-1**.

In Online and offline mode, the application id is auto increment by 1, so the next saved or submitted application will be **2021-ROB-MICH-2, 2021-ROB-MICH-3** …..

The code will be shown in the app while filling the pre-test or post test application inside the **Child’s code field** and after the collector submits the application in the post-pending page, the last item (U can use the date of the application as an indicator).

# Data collection, storage and reporting

Data should be collected through Android tablets by downloading the measuring wellbeing app (https://measuringwellbeing.org/apk/sdq-10.apk ) provided by UNICEF. Data collection takes approximately 15-20 minutes.

Data can be collected by one person only, in a face-to-face interview, in a confidential space. However, and as mentioned above a facilitator should never remain alone in a closed space with a child. Facilitators should have an extra paper form with them if they are using a tablet, in case of technical problems.

  - Caza

  - Cadastral

  - Site type (e.g. village, informal settlement, school, community centers, SDC, municipalities, etc.…)

  - Age (restricted according to each form: between 6-11 or 12-17),

  - Gender to be selected from a dropdown list

  - Nationality to be selected from a dropdown list

  - Child labour

  - Child Marriage

  - Violent discipline

  - Other

There will be a box for the total number of hours in the program and a separate box for the total number of hours that specific child attended. For example, if there are 20 sessions in the total program and each session is 2 hours, the total number of hours will be 40. Then, from the attendance sheet you will know that child attended 15 sessions, so you will enter 30 hours into the second box.

- The final page marks the end of the interview. It is important to make sure that the form is marked as finalized prior to submitting it.

**<u>Scoring and analysis</u>**

<u>For the purposes of the LCRP Outcome 4 indicator C, the definition for “increase in psychosocial wellbeing” will be the equivalent of the number of children whose overall stress score decreased, even by one point.</u> Further analysis will be done based on the mean total stress score in the pre- and post-assessment, in order to understand if the change was significant or not. Furthermore, each of the 5 sub-scales will be analysed according to the internationally recognized categories of “normal”, “borderline” and “abnormal”. While it is a limitation as the categories have not been contextualized for Lebanon, the intent is not to make a diagnosis but to provide different ways to analyse the data. The number of contact hours will also be considered.

**<u>Identification and referral</u>**

For serious concerns regarding the mental health of any child participating in PSS activities that arise during activities or through the administration of the SDQ, please contact your supervisor or follow the relevant referral pathway in your location.
